How the budget works (read this)

Every scenario gets the same monthly budget and the same starting capital. Each year: housing cost (mortgage/rent/upkeep) is paid first, and whatever is left of the budget is invested into stocks/index at the return above. So the model already does both things automatically — a cheaper-housing scenario simply has more left over to invest, and that compounding is counted. "Rent then buy" invests the surplus while renting, then uses the grown pot as a bigger down payment. "Buy now" takes the loan and invests whatever the budget leaves after the mortgage. You don't choose between those — both are modelled; the comparison shows which comes out ahead.

Off = nominal euros (the actual number on a future bank statement). On = every figure is discounted back to today's purchasing power, so €1M in 30 years shows as what it could actually buy now (roughly €410k at 3% inflation). Use this to compare scenarios by real wealth, not inflated headline numbers.
